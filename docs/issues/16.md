# Issue #16 — Deliver a long transaction in parts instead of holding it whole

**GitHub:** [AxialDB/ce-stream#16](https://github.com/AxialDB/ce-stream/issues/16)
**Label:** (feature)
**Status:** **In progress** on branch `issue-16/transaction-parts`. Planned as **v0.5.0**, released after it is tested with its first consumer (AxialDB).

> **Resolution:** the MySQL and MongoDB readers deliver a transaction larger than one part (`part_max_rows` rows or `part_max_bytes` bytes of row data, defaults 10,000 and 16 MB) in parts, as they read it. Every part carries the transaction's GTID (MySQL) or the position of its first event (MongoDB), its number, and whether it is the last. Only the last part carries the position after the transaction, and only it moves the checkpoint. A transaction of one part or less is delivered whole, as before.

The **Problem summary** below is the problem as reported. It is not current runtime behavior.

## Problem summary

The MySQL reader kept every row of a transaction until the transaction ended (`TxnBuffer`), one CloudEvent per row, and delivered it whole. One `UPDATE` of every row of a large table was held whole in memory: on a consumer's machine with 32 GB, a 2,000,000-row `UPDATE` (4.6 GB of binlog) exhausted it. The MongoDB reader did the same with a multi-document transaction (measured by the consumer: 7 to 10 KB per document, 2 GB for 200,000 documents).

## Behavior

- **Parts.** A part ends at `part_max_rows` rows or at `part_max_bytes` bytes of row data (`approx_json_bytes`), whichever comes first. A full part goes out when the next row of its transaction comes, so a transaction of exactly one part is delivered whole, with no empty last part. A row larger than `part_max_bytes` is a part of its own. Rows the include list leaves out do not count. Both limits are settings: `SourceConfig::part_max_rows` / `part_max_bytes` in the library, `part_max_rows` / `part_max_bytes` under `[source]` in the CLI config (0 is refused there).
- **The part fields.** `CommittedTransaction::part` is `Some(TransactionPart { number, last })` for a transaction delivered in parts and `None` for a whole one; `ends_transaction()` says whether a delivery ends its transaction. Avro: a part uses `committed-transaction-v3` (v2 plus `part_number` and `part_last`); whole transactions stay on v1 (MySQL) or v2.
- **MySQL.** Every part carries the transaction's GTID. Only the last part carries `gtid_set_after`, and its rows the `gtidset` extension; the rows of other parts do not. `DDL` of `CREATE TABLE ... SELECT` stays in the first part, in front of its rows.
- **MongoDB.** The parts of a multi-document transaction carry the position of its first event as `at` and an empty `after`; the last part carries the resume token after the transaction. A transaction larger than 16 MB is a chain of oplog entries in MongoDB, all written at its commit, and the change stream shows its events as one run with one `(lsid, txnNumber)`: the reader sees one transaction, however many oplog entries it has (checked on 8.0.32 and 9.0.2).
- **The queue to the consumer.** The queue between the reader and the consumer holds at most `queue_capacity` deliveries (default 64) and at most `queue_max_bytes` bytes of row data (default 32 MB, two parts); one delivery always fits an empty queue. Without the byte limit, a consumer slower than the reader could have 64 parts waiting, about 1 GB of CloudEvents (measured with AxialDB: about 375 MB more for one 1,000,000-row `UPDATE`). `SourceConfig::queue_max_bytes`, and `queue_max_bytes` under `[source]` in the CLI config (0 is refused there). MySQL; the MongoDB reader delivers in its own task and holds no queue.
- **Checkpoint.** Only a delivery that ends its transaction moves it. A restart in the middle of a transaction reads it again from its start.
- **`ROLLBACK`.** MySQL writes a transaction to the binlog only when it commits; with `gtid_mode=ON` (a capture gate) a transaction never ends with `ROLLBACK` there (checked on 9.7: an InnoDB rollback writes nothing, a MyISAM one is written as a commit, both in one transaction are refused). Should one come all the same after parts of its transaction were delivered, the reader stops with an error that names the transaction; before any part it is not delivered, as before. An aborted MongoDB transaction never reaches the change stream.
- **A delivery the consumer refuses** ends the stream with the consumer's error at once. Before, the MySQL reader then waited for its binlog thread, which could be blocked handing the next delivery to a full queue: the stream hung until the server dropped the connection. Found while testing parts, which fill the queue quickly.

## Non-goals (this issue)

- Changing how a transaction smaller than one part is delivered.
- Changing the CloudEvent row format beyond the part fields and the executed set (MySQL) moving to the last part.
- MongoDB writes that are not one transaction (an `updateMany` outside a transaction is one change per document and is delivered so already).

## Tests

- Unit, MySQL (`crates/ce-stream-mysql/src/txn_buffer.rs`, `dispatch.rs`, `tests/transaction_shapes.rs`): cut by rows and by bytes; a transaction of exactly one part is whole, one row more is two parts; parts across row events; `CREATE TABLE ... SELECT` keeps its statement in the first part; the transaction after one in parts is whole again; only the last part moves the checkpoint; a `ROLLBACK` after a delivered part stops the stream, before any part it is not delivered.
- Unit, MongoDB (`crates/ce-stream-mongo/src/group.rs`): the same by rows and by bytes; a short transaction and a single write stay whole; rows the include list leaves out do not fill a part.
- Unit, core: `a_part_roundtrips_avro_v3`.
- Unit, the queue (`crates/ce-stream-mysql/src/queue.rs`): the reader waits until the queue has room; one delivery fits an empty queue whatever its size; a waiting reader ends when the consumer is gone.
- Crash harness (`scripts/crash-harness/`, not in CI): `-PartMaxRows N` / `--part-max-rows N` kills ce-stream after the whole first part was taken; the checkpoint has not moved and the restart delivers the transaction again from part 1. `ce-stream-perf-sink --stall-at N` hangs on the Nth delivery, so transaction mode with a whole transaction is tested too (it could not hang the first delivery before). All four cases (row and transaction mode, whole and in parts of 3) passed on MySQL 9.7 on Windows on 2026-10-15/16.
- With AxialDB (not in CI): one `UPDATE` of 200,000 to 2,000,000 rows on MySQL 9.7 through the engine, and a live table created while such a transaction comes in.
