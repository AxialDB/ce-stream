# Release v0.5.0 — Long transactions in parts

**Date:** (not released)
**Tag:** [`v0.5.0`](https://github.com/AxialDB/ce-stream/releases/tag/v0.5.0)

## Summary

Implements [#16](https://github.com/AxialDB/ce-stream/issues/16). A transaction that changes millions of rows was held whole in memory by the reader, and delivered whole to the consumer, which held it whole too. The MySQL and MongoDB readers now deliver a long transaction in parts, as they read it, so that the memory a transaction needs is bounded by the size of a part. Transactions of one part or less are delivered as before.

## Added

- **Parts.** A part ends at `part_max_rows` rows (default 10,000) or `part_max_bytes` bytes of row data (default 16 MB), whichever comes first. Both are settings: `SourceConfig::part_max_rows` / `part_max_bytes`, and the same keys under `[source]` in the CLI config.
- **`queue_max_bytes`** (MySQL): the deliveries waiting for the consumer hold at most this many bytes of row data, besides `queue_capacity` deliveries. Default 32 MB, two parts; one delivery always fits an empty queue. `SourceConfig::queue_max_bytes`, and the same key under `[source]` in the CLI config. Without it, 64 parts could wait for a slow consumer.
- **`CommittedTransaction::part`**: `Some(TransactionPart { number, last })` on a part, `None` on a whole transaction. `CommittedTransaction::ends_transaction()` says whether a delivery ends its transaction.
- **Avro `committed-transaction-v3`** for a part: the fields of v2 plus `part_number` and `part_last`. Whole transactions stay on v1 (MySQL) or v2.
- **`ce_stream_core::event::approx_json_bytes`**, the measure of a row's size that bounds a part, for both readers.
- **`ce-stream-perf-sink --stall-at N`**: hang on the Nth delivery, the first one included. The sink's stats count deliveries that hang (`stalled`).
- **Crash harness `-PartMaxRows N` / `--part-max-rows N`**: the kill falls between two parts of one transaction.

## Changed

- **A long transaction arrives as several deliveries.** A consumer that applies every delivery as a whole transaction has to read `part`: only a delivery that ends its transaction is a commit. A part that is not the last carries no position after the transaction (MySQL: empty `gtid_set_after`; MongoDB: empty `after`), and the checkpoint does not move with it. A restart in the middle reads the transaction again from its start, from part 1.
- **MySQL: the executed set is on the last part only**, on the delivery and on its rows (`gtidset`). The rows of other parts carry `gtid` and not `gtidset`.
- **MySQL: a `ROLLBACK` after a delivered part stops the stream** with an error that names the transaction. MySQL with `gtid_mode=ON` does not write one; before any part, such a transaction is not delivered, as before.

## Fixed

- **MySQL: a delivery the consumer refused could hang the stream.** The reader waited for its binlog thread, which could be blocked handing the next delivery to a full queue; the error came back only after the server dropped the connection. The queue is now closed first, and the error comes back at once.

## What changes for a consumer

- Read `part` (or call `ends_transaction()`) before treating a delivery as a commit. Hold or apply the parts of a transaction as they come; treat it as committed at its last part.
- When a delivery that is not the next part of the transaction you hold comes (part 1 again, or another transaction), drop the parts you hold: the reader started the stream again and sends that transaction from its start.
- A consumer that stores its position per delivery stores none for a part that is not the last.

## Not supported, as before

- XA transactions: the rows between `XA START` and `XA PREPARE` are not delivered.
